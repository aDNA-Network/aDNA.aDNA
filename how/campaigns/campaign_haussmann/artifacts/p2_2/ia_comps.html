Option A — consolidate to /use-cases/

The narrative surface wins: richer prose, the more conventional public label, the larger set. Redirect-only — no content rewrite. Every audience keeps a browsable page; the reader can still self-select.

For

  • Redirect-only. The surviving pages are the ones already written best; nothing is re-authored, so the risk is close to zero.
  • Keeps the self-select surface. A first-time visitor can still scan "is this for me?" — a real acquisition asset.
  • Most reversible of the three if the ranker or later evidence disagrees.
  • Solves the enterprise routing gap: /enterprise folds into a nav-reachable page.

Against

  • "Use cases" is a marketing register on a site whose positioning is deliberately anti-marketing.
  • It describes scenarios where the reader often wants instructions.
  • Keeps an identity taxonomy — the thing 7.7 warns is a substitute for positioning — merely deduplicated.
  • Nav still carries 7; no headroom for a future section.

Option C — retire into positioned sections (task taxonomy)

Both branches retire. The site stops sorting readers by who they are and sorts work by what they are trying to do — which is what the positioning promises: "always know where things live". Scenario narratives survive as evidence attached to a task, not as segments.

For

  • The only option that matches ADR-048's own self-description — standard, docs, registry — at the top level.
  • Answers 7.7 rather than deduplicating it. The identity taxonomy is retired, not tidied.
  • Smallest nav (6) with genuine headroom; largest reduction in surface.
  • Task-shaped content is what a reader arriving from an agentic tool actually wants.

Against

  • Largest move — 18 URLs and 13 pages re-framed, so the most exposure in one commit.
  • Deletes the self-select surface. A visitor who wants to see themselves on the page no longer can.
  • Least reversible; the prose is re-framed, not just re-homed.
  • Concentrates weight on /get-started/, a page not yet built to carry four paths.

Option B (consolidate to /adopters/) was cut at O0, not omitted. It is dominated on the evidence: it costs more redirects (12) and a substantial content rewrite, because it retires the richer prose into the thinner shells — and it has no home for open-source-project, which has no adopter twin. "Adopters" is also insider vocabulary; a stranger does not self-identify as one. Full reasoning in ADR-049 §Option B.
