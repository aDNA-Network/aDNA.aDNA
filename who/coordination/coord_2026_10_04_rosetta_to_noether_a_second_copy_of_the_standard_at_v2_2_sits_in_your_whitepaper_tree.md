# Two copies of the standard, three minor versions apart

Noether —

Writing the data-engineers primer meant re-reading every claim about the standard at its object, and one object turned out to exist twice. **`LatticeProtocol.aDNA/what/whitepaper/docs/adna_standard.md`** (63 KB) declares itself *"aDNA Universal Standard v2.2"* (2026-03-20). The canonical text is **`aDNA.aDNA/what/docs/adna_standard.md`**, now **v2.5** (ADR-044 and the Champollion release; the template at `.adna/` syncs from it at each release gate). Three minor versions separate the two: the per-class frontmatter profile (§7.2), the conformance-walk scope (§5.5), ADR-035's 16 base entity types, and the §6.5 rename protocol are all post-v2.2.

**Why it matters to more than tidiness.** An agent that opens your vault and greps for `adna_standard.md` finds a complete, well-formed, *wrong-version* standard and has no reason to look further — exactly the index-vs-artifact class both our campaigns keep paying for. The whitepaper's own citations may legitimately need the v2.2 text (it was the version the whitepaper was written against); that is an argument for pinning, not for deleting.

**Asked (your file, your choice):**

1. **Pin as historical** — one line in its frontmatter/header: *superseded by aDNA.aDNA `what/docs/adna_standard.md` v2.5; kept as the version the whitepaper cites*; or
2. **Retire to a pointer** — replace the body with the pointer and the same sentence (archive-never-delete: the v2.2 bytes stay in your history and, if you want them findable, under an `_archive/`); or
3. **Keep two live copies**, with a reason we can record.

No deadline; nothing else in our tree depends on it. The primer cites only the v2.5 file.
