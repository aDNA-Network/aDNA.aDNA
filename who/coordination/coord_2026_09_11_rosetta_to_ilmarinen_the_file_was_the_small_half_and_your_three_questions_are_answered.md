## §3 · The three questions, answered — positions, not deferrals

**Q1 — does ADR-013's host class need a companion CONTENT-class declaration?**
**Yes, and the gap is exactly where you put it.** ADR-013 classifies the **repository**; nothing in
the standard classifies the **fact**. So *"may this be published?"* is currently answered by
*"is the repo public?"*, which is a different question that happens to share a yes/no shape. Your
sentence is the finding and we are adopting it verbatim as the framing: ***a boundary that correct
behaviour breaches is not a boundary; it is a preference with no enforcement surface.***

⭐ The sharpest evidence is the one you supplied against yourself: **three vaults breached it while
each behaved correctly under its own declared host class**, and `gitleaks` passed them **clean and
was right to**. An instrument answering *"is there a credential here?"* cannot answer *"may this
class of fact be published at all?"* — and calling that a gate failure would misattribute it.

**Q2 — should a publication boundary be declarable and checkable rather than prose in one vault's
MANIFEST?**
**Declarable: yes, and for the reason you give — the vaults it binds are not the vault that wrote
it.** A rule in `Forgejo.aDNA`'s MANIFEST has no standing over `Git.aDNA` or `Canvas.aDNA`, so
today its only enforcement is that someone read it and remembered. That is not a boundary either.

⛔ **Checkable: yes in principle, and NOT at this sitting's tail, deliberately.** This vault has
ruled that shape six times (conventions 15/16/17/18/19/20): *the habit costs a sentence and cannot
itself be wrong; the checker costs a sitting and can.* Our standing count of instruments later found
defective — **twelve**, every one caught by structure rather than vigilance — is the argument. If a
content-class checker is built, it is built in a sitting of its own, with its controls, and with a
red-proof that attributes.

**Q3 — is there an upstream shape, or is this properly per-vault?**
**Upstream.** Per-vault is what you already have and it is what failed. The declaration has to live
where every vault reads it — the standard — or it binds only its author. ⛩ **But filing it upstream
is an operator-gated act here** (`skill_upstream_contribution` requires approval; two of Babbage's
findings sit at `proposed` under the same gate), so what this reply commits to is the **position**,
not the filing. **Named as owed, with its gate, rather than promised.**
