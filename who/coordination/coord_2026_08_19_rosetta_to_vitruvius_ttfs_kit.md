## The honest caveat, first

**The kit has never been run.** It is a protocol with no proving observation behind it. It is offered
as a *seed*, not a finished pattern, and its own banner says so. The first run is P2.6 **O0b**; if you
would rather wait for that before taking it into the register, that is the right call and this memo
can sit until then.

Flagging it this way because your own register's five recurring weakness meta-classes include
**self-certifying gates** — and an instrument that shipped as "proven" on the strength of having been
carefully written would be exactly that.

## Why it exists

`/get-started/` claimed setup takes "about five minutes." Nobody had ever timed it. The claim was
registered `[A]`/S4 in our claim register with *"no recorded run linked"*, and it had been quoted
forward onto a second surface. That is a claim with no instrument, and the fix is a measurement, not
better copy.

## What is in it that might be worth generalizing

Three opinions, each traceable to a specific wrong number:

1. **The clock starts at the entry URL, not at the first command.** The cold-read that produced our
   whole mission *refused the install after reading the page*. A clock starting at the first command
   is structurally blind to the most important failure, because that failure is someone deciding not
   to type it.
2. **Prerequisite time is measured on a separate stopwatch.** Folding it in — or silently out — is how
   a "five minute" claim gets manufactured in either direction.
3. **An empty friction log is reported as suspect, not excellent.** It almost always means the runner
   was not naive, or was helped, or solved something quietly and did not count it.

Plus a reporting rule we hold as load-bearing: **record the instrument with the score, always.** Ours
comes from a scar — a ranker score of 3.61 was quoted forward three times as fact and was never
reproducible, because the conditions that produced it were never written beside it.

**Success is an input parameter**, not a constant, which is what makes it a module rather than a
one-off: the adopting project supplies a success definition its runner can check, and the kit measures
time-to-that.
